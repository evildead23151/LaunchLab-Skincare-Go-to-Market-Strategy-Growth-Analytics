# Project Charter — LaunchLab

## Project identity

- Project: LaunchLab — Minimalist Ingredient-Clarity GTM Case Study
- Type: Independent product-marketing and go-to-market portfolio project
- Brand studied: Minimalist (Uprising Science)
- Audience hypothesis: College students and first-time skincare buyers aged 18–24 in India
- Working campaign platform: The Ingredient Clarity Edit
- Owner: Gitesh Malik
- Status: Initial desk research and framing

## Background

First-time skincare buyers may encounter ingredient names, concentrations, multiple product types, claims, reviews and routine advice without knowing how to interpret them. The project examines whether clearer educational content and a guided decision journey could improve understanding and confidence.

Minimalist already publicly communicates ingredient transparency and offers Skin Insights and curated kits. Competitors also provide ingredient education and skin-assessment features. The project must identify an incremental opportunity rather than assume these assets are absent.

## Objective

Develop an evidence-led campaign and GTM recommendation for an existing Indian skincare brand. Demonstrate consumer research, competitor intelligence, positioning, messaging, channel planning, campaign execution and measurement.

## Scope

**In scope**
- Public market and competitor research.
- Primary consumer research with voluntary participation and appropriate consent.
- Audit of public brand and product communication.
- Audience segmentation and positioning.
- Messaging and campaign concept development.
- Channel and launch strategy.
- Landing-page/content experiment where feasible.
- Marketing KPI definitions and retrospective.

**Out of scope**
- Product formulation or medical advice.
- Unsupported health, safety or efficacy claims.
- Representing or speaking for Minimalist.
- Internal brand data, customer lists, ad accounts or confidential information.
- Claims of sales lift, ROAS, CAC or conversion gains without attributable measurement.

## Key hypotheses

- H1: Ingredient disclosure does not always translate into beginner understanding.
- H2: First-time buyers may need help connecting product information to a basic routine decision.
- H3: Trust signals and evidence clarity may influence consideration alongside price and creator recommendations.
- H4: Educational content may encourage more qualified product exploration.

Each hypothesis must be tested and may be rejected.

## Planned research targets

- Consumer survey: 50–75 responses (target, not achieved).
- Qualitative interviews: 8–12 participants (target, not achieved).
- Competitor set: five initial brands.
- Campaign test: one landing page and a small set of message/content variants, if feasible.

These targets do not make a convenience sample representative.

## Deliverables

1. Dated source register.
2. Competitor benchmark and positioning map.
3. Survey and interview research, with anonymised synthesis.
4. Audience segmentation and positioning brief.
5. Messaging framework and creative briefs.
6. GTM strategy deck and campaign calendar.
7. Landing page/content experiment and results, if run.
8. KPI dictionary and final retrospective.

## Success criteria

Success means a traceable decision process and defensible evidence—not impressive-looking invented outcomes. The final report should explain what was learned, what changed, what failed, and what remains unknown.

## Constraints and ethics

- Respect participant consent and privacy.
- Do not collect unnecessary health or sensitive personal information.
- Do not publish identifying quotes without explicit permission.
- Cite public sources and retain access dates.
- Label assumptions and synthetic data.
- Do not reproduce copyrighted competitor creatives or claim brand affiliation.
